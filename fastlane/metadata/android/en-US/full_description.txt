Hang a lucky charm on your screen and give it a pull.

My Lucky Charm puts a charm on a rope at the top of your display. It hangs
there, it sways, and it swings when you flick it — a real rope simulation, not
an animation on a loop. Drag it out to the side and let go, and it whips back
the way a charm on a cord actually would.

<b>Pick your charm</b>
Seventeen charms are built in — a lucky cat, a horseshoe, an evil eye, a hamsa,
a scarab, a daruma, an oni mask, a temple bell, a lucky knot, an ash gourd, a
string of chillies and lemon, two om charms, a moon and star, a 786 medallion,
and two crosses. Prefer an emoji? There is a curated palette of them, each one
named so you are not guessing at a tiny glyph, and you can type any other emoji
your keyboard offers. Or hang a photo of your own: pick one and it goes
straight onto the rope.

<b>Pull it to do something</b>
A pull can be just a pull. Or you can set it to control screen brightness or
media volume — pull down for more, push up for less. It adjusts from wherever
the setting already is, so grabbing the charm in the middle of a video nudges
the volume rather than resetting it. A sideways swing leaves it alone entirely.

Tapping does something too, if you want it to: set a single, double, or triple
tap on the charm to toggle the flashlight or mute the sound.

<b>Keep it while you use other apps</b>
Floating mode hangs the charm over everything else on your screen. It is
genuinely see-through: every tap except one on the charm itself goes to the app
underneath, so nothing is in your way. A notification with a stop button is
always there while it runs.

<b>Make it yours</b>
Drag the charm's anchor anywhere along the top. Tune the rope length, the charm
size, and how far the rope stretches, and watch it change under your finger as
you drag the slider. Turn on tilt and the charm swings with the way you hold
your phone.

<b>No account, no ads, no network</b>
This F-Droid build asks for no internet permission at all. There is no
sign-in, no ads, and no analytics of any kind — the analytics SDK is not merely
switched off, it is absent from the build. Your settings and any photo you
choose stay in the app's private storage on your device, and uninstalling
removes them.
